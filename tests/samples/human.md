The plumber turned up at seven and left at ten past. That was the whole job, apparently. A new washer, a bit of tape, forty-five dollars.

I'd been dreading it for three weeks. The tap in the downstairs bathroom had been dripping since the end of March, and every time I went to ring someone I found a reason not to. Too busy. Too expensive. Probably it would fix itself, which taps famously do not.

My wife had been counting the drips for fun. Eleven a minute, she said, though I never checked. Somewhere around week two she stopped mentioning it and just put a mug under the spout, which was worse than nagging.

So when Ravi finally knocked, I had a speech ready about how it was probably the whole fitting, how I understood if he'd need to order parts, how I wasn't in a hurry. He looked at the tap for maybe four seconds. Said "washer," like a doctor naming a cold. Then he got on with it.

I paid him in cash and stood in the hallway feeling foolish. Three weeks of low-grade guilt, over a rubber ring that cost less than a sandwich.

The mug is still under the tap. Nobody's moved it. I think we both like the reminder.
